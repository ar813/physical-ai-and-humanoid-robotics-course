# Glossary

This glossary contains definitions of technical terms used throughout the Physical AI & Humanoid Robotics course. Terms are organized alphabetically for easy reference.

## A

**Actuator**  
A device that creates movement in a robot. Examples include motors, servos, and hydraulic systems.

**AI (Artificial Intelligence)**  
The simulation of human intelligence in machines that are programmed to think and learn.

**Algorithm**  
A step-by-step procedure or formula for solving a problem or completing a task.

**ASIMO**  
Advanced Step in Innovative Mobility - a humanoid robot created by Honda.

**Autonomous**  
Able to operate independently without human control.

**Action (ROS)**  
A type of communication in ROS used for long-running tasks that provide feedback and can be preempted.

## B

**Bipedal**  
Having two feet or legs. Humanoid robots are typically bipedal.

**Boston Dynamics**  
A robotics company known for advanced robots like Atlas and Spot.

## C

**Camera**  
A sensor that captures visual information, allowing robots to "see."

**Center of Mass (COM)**  
The point where the total mass of a robot is considered to be concentrated, critical for balance.

**Collision Detection**  
The process of identifying when objects in a simulation or real world come into contact.

**Computer Vision**  
The field of AI that enables computers and robots to understand and interpret visual information.

**Control System**  
The brain of a robot that processes sensor data and controls actuators.

## D

**DDS (Data Distribution Service)**  
The middleware protocol used by ROS 2 for real-time data communication between nodes.

**Deep Learning**  
A subset of machine learning using neural networks with multiple layers.

**Depth Sensor**  
A sensor that measures the distance to objects, creating a 3D map of the environment.

**DOF (Degrees of Freedom)**  
The number of independent movements a robot or robot part can make.

## E

**Encoder**  
A sensor that measures the rotation of a motor or joint, providing position feedback.

**End Effector**  
The device at the end of a robotic arm (e.g., gripper, tool, or hand).

**Environment**  
The physical space where a robot operates.

## F

**Forward Kinematics**  
Calculating the position and orientation of a robot's end effector from its joint angles.

**Feedback**  
Information from sensors that helps a robot adjust its actions.

**Force Sensor**  
A sensor that measures the amount of force applied to an object.

## G

**Gear Ratio**  
The ratio between the rotational speeds of two or more interlocking gears, affecting torque and speed.

**Gait**  
The pattern of movement when walking or running.

**Gazebo**  
A popular open-source robot simulation software.

**GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer)**  
A type of large language model used for natural language processing.

**Gripper**  
A robot end effector designed to grasp and hold objects.

## H

**Homogeneous Coordinates**  
A mathematical representation used in robotics to describe transformations in 3D space.

**Humanoid Robot**  
A robot with a human-like body structure, typically with a head, torso, two arms, and two legs.

**HRI (Human-Robot Interaction)**  
The study of how humans and robots communicate and work together.

## I

**Inverse Kinematics**  
Calculating the joint angles needed to achieve a desired end effector position and orientation.

**IMU (Inertial Measurement Unit)**  
A sensor that measures acceleration, rotation, and orientation.

**Isaac Sim**  
NVIDIA's robot simulation platform for AI and robotics development.

## J

**Joint**  
A connection point between two robot parts that allows movement.

## K

**Kinematics**  
The study of motion without considering forces. In robotics, it describes how robot parts move.

## L

**Launch File**  
A configuration file in ROS that starts multiple nodes and sets parameters automatically.

**LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging)**  
A sensor that uses laser light to measure distances and create detailed 3D maps.

**Locomotion**  
The ability to move from one place to another.

## M

**Machine Learning**  
A type of AI that allows systems to learn and improve from experience.

**Manipulation**  
The ability to handle and move objects.

**Mesh**  
A 3D model representation of an object made up of vertices, edges, and faces used in simulation.

**Motor**  
An actuator that converts electrical energy into rotational motion.

## N

**Navigation**  
The process of planning and following a path to reach a destination.

**Neural Network**  
A computing system inspired by biological neural networks, used in AI.

**Node (ROS)**  
A process that performs computation in the Robot Operating System.

## O

**Odometry**  
The use of sensor data to estimate a robot's position and orientation.

**Optimus**  
Tesla's humanoid robot project.

## P

**Path Planning**  
The process of finding a route from one location to another while avoiding obstacles.

**Physics Engine**  
Software that simulates physical interactions like gravity, collisions, and friction in virtual environments.

**Physical AI**  
Artificial intelligence that interacts with and manipulates the physical world through robots.

**Policy**  
In machine learning, a strategy that maps observations to actions in reinforcement learning.

**Pose**  
The position and orientation of a robot or object in space.

**PWM (Pulse Width Modulation)**  
A technique for controlling motor speed and power by varying the width of electrical pulses.

**Python**  
A popular programming language widely used in robotics and AI.

## Q

**Quaternion**  
A mathematical representation of rotation in 3D space.

## R

**Reinforcement Learning**  
A machine learning approach where agents learn by receiving rewards or penalties for actions.

**Reward Function**  
In reinforcement learning, a function that assigns numerical rewards to states or actions.

**ROS (Robot Operating System)**  
An open-source framework for robot software development.

**ROS 2**  
The second generation of ROS with improved performance and real-time capabilities.

## S

**Service (ROS)**  
A request-response communication pattern in ROS for synchronous operations.

**SDF (Simulation Description Format)**  
An XML format for describing robot models and environments in simulation.

**Sensor**  
A device that detects and measures physical properties from the environment.

**Servo Motor**  
A motor with built-in position feedback for precise control.

**Simulation**  
A virtual environment where robots can be tested without physical hardware.

**SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping)**  
A technique for building a map while tracking the robot's location.

## T

**Topic (ROS)**  
A named channel for publishing and subscribing to messages in ROS.

**Torque**  
A rotational force applied by motors to move robot joints.

**Trajectory**  
A planned path that includes position, velocity, and acceleration over time.

**Transform**  
A mathematical representation of the position and orientation of one coordinate frame relative to another.

**Transfer Learning**  
A machine learning technique where knowledge from one task is applied to a different but related task.

**TypeScript**  
A programming language that adds types to JavaScript.

## U

**Unity**  
A game engine also used for robot simulation and visualization.

**URDF (Unified Robot Description Format)**  
An XML format for describing robot models in ROS.

## V

**VLA (Vision-Language-Action)**  
An AI model that combines visual perception, language understanding, and physical actions.

**Voice Command**  
Spoken instructions given to a robot or AI system.

## W

**Walking**  
The act of moving on legs, a key capability for humanoid robots.

## X

**XML (eXtensible Markup Language)**  
A markup language used in URDF and SDF files.

## Y

**YAML (YAML Ain't Markup Language)**  
A human-readable data format often used for configuration files.

## Z

**Zero Moment Point (ZMP)**  
A critical point on the ground where the sum of all tipping moments equals zero, essential for bipedal walking stability.

**Zero Position**  
The default or home position of a robot or robot joint.
